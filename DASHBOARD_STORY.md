## Narrative 1: For the City Ops Lead

### Headline
Bengaluru has the highest SLA breach rate of any city — more than double Delhi NCR's, the best-performing city.

### Evidence
Across the reconciled dataset of 600 bookings, Bengaluru recorded 17 SLA breaches out of 107 bookings, a breach rate of **15.89%**. This is the highest of all six cities. At the other end, Delhi NCR recorded just 6 breaches out of 83 bookings, a breach rate of **7.23%** — the lowest in the network, and less than half of Bengaluru's rate. The remaining four cities cluster in between: Hyderabad (14.29%), Mumbai (14.13%), Chennai (13.27%), and Pune (13.08%), all closer to the network-wide average SLA Breach Rate of 13.17% shown on the dashboard's KPI panel.

### Implication
Bengaluru's SLA performance needs immediate operational attention — a breach rate this far above both the network average and Delhi NCR's benchmark points to a partner-capacity, scheduling, or dispatch issue specific to that city rather than a category-wide problem. The City Ops Lead should prioritize a root-cause review of Bengaluru's partner scheduling and turnaround times, and could use Delhi NCR's operating model as a reference point for what's achievable.

## Narrative 2: For the Category Lead

### Headline
Deep Home Cleaning is the dominant revenue driver, generating more than 3x the revenue of the next-largest category, while Salon for Men is comparatively under-booked.

### Evidence
Deep Home Cleaning generated **₹5,08,964** in revenue from **176 bookings** — both the highest revenue and the highest booking volume of any category, and more than three times the revenue of the second-place category, Salon for Women (₹1,51,689 from 65 bookings). At the other end, Salon for Men generated the lowest revenue of any active category at **₹73,563** from **78 bookings**, despite having a comparable booking count to several higher-revenue categories — reflecting its lower average price band (₹349–₹1,499) rather than low demand.

### Implication
Deep Home Cleaning's outsized contribution makes it the category to protect and invest in first — partner capacity and SLA reliability here have an outsized effect on total company revenue. Salon for Men's low revenue despite reasonable booking volume suggests upsell or bundling opportunities (e.g., promoting higher-value add-on services) rather than a demand problem, and is worth testing before assuming the category needs deprioritizing.
